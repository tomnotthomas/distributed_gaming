{{brand}} OS: a dual boot next to your Windows

Guests never play in your Windows. They play in a separate system built for exactly this.

  • A trusted start

    {{brand}} OS is built on Ubuntu LTS and only starts signed. Secure Boot checks the boot, the system itself is read-only and verified on every start, and the TPM chip lets your PC prove it is running exactly this system. That's why we need Secure Boot and TPM 2.0.

  • Kept off your home network

    Guests get the internet, not your home network: devices on your LAN and your router's admin page are blocked. The game and the stream run over your connection, just as when you play online yourself.

  • Your drives

    {{brand}} OS never writes to your Windows drives. Your Steam drive is mounted read-only, so guests can use your installed games without downloading them again. No other drive is mounted, and USB sticks are blocked.

  • Fresh after every guest

    After every session the PC restarts and wipes everything the guest left behind, including their Steam login.

  • Space and BitLocker

    {{brand}} OS takes a fixed 24 GB. Your Steam drive can't be encrypted with BitLocker, or {{brand}} OS can't read it. Note down your BitLocker recovery key first: Windows sometimes asks for it after you turn on Secure Boot.

  • Removing it

    An uninstall that removes {{brand}} OS and its boot entry completely will be ready before the beta starts.

What you get as a Founding Host

  • Seats for your friends

    Friends who use your link play on your PC, with a seat saved for them even when the beta is full.

  • Your host page

    A Founding Host badge and your own host page: your specs, hours hosted and how many players you've helped. Under a nickname, never your address.

  • Your idle PC, put to good use

    Instead of sitting idle, it lets someone without a gaming PC play their own Steam library. And you get a say in how {{brand}} grows from day one.

Hosting: your questions

Can guests get to my files?

No. Guests play in {{brand}} OS, a separate, locked-down system. {{brand}} OS never writes to your Windows drives: your Steam drive is read-only, and it doesn't mount any other drive. The PC restarts after every session. How {{brand}} OS works

What happens to my Windows?

It stays as it is. {{brand}} OS takes a fixed 24 GB: free space on the disk, or it shrinks a drive that keeps at least 16 GB free. When you're not sharing, your PC starts Windows as usual.

Who plays on my PC?

At first only friends you invite. They sign in with their own Steam account and play their own games. Whether Mac players from our waitlist join later is up to you.

How much electricity does it use?

About as much as playing on it yourself, and only while a guest is playing. A gaming PC draws roughly 300 to 450 watts under load; at 40 cents per kWh that's 12 to 18 cents an hour. Two three-hour test sessions come to about one euro a month.

Can I take my PC back at any time?

Yes, right at the PC, as long as no guest is playing. If a session is running, you wait until it ends.

I only run Linux on my PC. Does that work?

Not yet. {{brand}} OS is installed from Windows and switches back to Windows afterwards, so you need a Windows gaming PC. Sign up anyway and we'll let you know if that changes.

Is this like Steam Remote Play?

Similar. With Remote Play you stream your PC to your own phone or laptop. With {{brand}} your PC streams to a Mac player who plays their own games on their own Steam account, inside {{brand}} OS, not in your Windows.

Is {{brand}} OS a dual boot?

Yes. {{brand}} OS gets its own 24 GB next to Windows. To share, your PC starts {{brand}} OS, and afterwards it goes back to Windows.

How do I turn on Secure Boot?

In your motherboard's BIOS, usually under "Boot" or "Security". Note down your BitLocker recovery key first. Our check shows whether Secure Boot is already on and walks you through it step by step, with guides for MSI, ASUS, Gigabyte and ASRock.
