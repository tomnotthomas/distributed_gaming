Invites

When you invite someone, they see your Steam name and what the invite is for (for example, a seat at your PC). When you share your crew link, the link preview (for example, in WhatsApp) shows your Steam name. We store nothing about the invited person until they sign in with Steam themselves.

Error reports from Lanterel Host and Lanterel OS

When Lanterel Host or a Lanterel OS service on your PC hits an error, it sends an error report to PostHog ([POSTHOG ENTITY, LOCATION], servers in the EU), with whom we have a data processing agreement. The report holds the kind of error, its message, where in the program's code it happened, and which program sent it, in which version. Before it leaves the PC, the program removes Steam IDs, e-mail and IP addresses, user names in file paths, invite links, keys and credentials from it. The report belongs to no profile, and we have no location looked up. Sending it, PostHog sees the PC's IP address, as any connection does. We use the reports to find and fix errors (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) and delete them after [PERIOD]. With the environment variable DO_NOT_TRACK set on the PC (for example DO_NOT_TRACK=1), the programs send no error reports.

Stored in your browser

If you switch the language, your browser remembers your choice (local storage, key "site-lang"). On your crew page, your browser remembers for the current session your crew code, whether you have a gaming PC and whether you've already shared your link (session storage, keys "crew-role", "crew-code", "crew-sent"). Both are used only for these purposes (§ 25(2) TDDDG).
