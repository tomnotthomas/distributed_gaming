How {{brand}} OS works

A small, separate system that sits alongside Windows. Guests only ever play inside it, and nobody gets near your Windows install.

    Check your PC

    In about a minute, the {{brand}} app tells you whether your PC can handle it, and what's missing if it can't.

  2. 2

    Save your BitLocker key

    If Windows is encrypted, the app won't install until you've saved your recovery key.

  3. 3

    Confirm the key once

    To make sure only our signed system can boot, you confirm our key once on a blue screen with an eight-digit code.

  4. 4

    Remove it in one click

    “Remove {{brand}} OS” deletes the 24 GB and the boot entry. After that, only Windows boots.

How your PC boots

  1. You decide what boots

    When you want to share, the {{brand}} app restarts your PC into {{brand}} OS, and later back into Windows. The rest of the time it boots just like it always has.

  2. Signed only

    Secure Boot only lets signed software start. You confirm our key once during install, with an eight-digit code.

  3. Checked on every boot

    The system is read-only and verified on every boot. The TPM chip lets your PC prove nothing has been changed.

For the nerds: systemd-boot loads a signed unified kernel image, the root partition is protected by dm-verity, and the boot is measured into PCR 11. Built with mkosi from Ubuntu 26.04 LTS.

What a guest gets, and what's off-limits.

They get

  • Steam in full screen, signed in to their own account
  • their own games, the ones they actually own
  • your Steam drive, read-only, so they don't have to download games again
  • the internet, through your connection

Off-limits

  • your Windows and your other drives
  • your home network: devices on your Wi-Fi, printers and your router's settings page
  • USB sticks and external drives
  • your Steam account, your passwords, your saves

Before you install: your BitLocker key

If Windows is encrypted with BitLocker, it may ask for your recovery key once after you turn on Secure Boot. The {{brand}} app checks this first and won't install until you have the key.

Your Steam drive needs to be unencrypted, otherwise {{brand}} OS can't read the games. Your other drives stay as they are.

Check it yourself in a Command Prompt run as administrator:

manage-bde -protectors -get C:

Or online in your Microsoft account:

aka.ms/myrecoverykey

Gone in one click

The {{brand}} app has a “Remove {{brand}} OS” button. It deletes the 24 GB and the boot entry, and your PC goes back to starting only Windows, just like before.
